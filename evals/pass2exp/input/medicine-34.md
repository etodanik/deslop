I'm sorry you're both going through this, but there's reassuring news: a torn frenulum is a **very common injury**, especially in uncircumcised men, and it typically heals well.

**Will sex be normal again?**

In most cases, **yes**. Once the tear has fully healed, most couples return to normal sexual function. Some men notice the area is a bit more sensitive or that the skin is tighter, and in some cases a small scar forms, which can occasionally cause discomfort or make it prone to tearing again.

**Will it cause erectile dysfunction or change fertility?**

- **Erectile dysfunction:** A frenulum tear doesn't affect the nerves or blood vessels responsible for erections. If a man has some temporary difficulty, it's usually psychological (anxiety about pain or re-injury), and it typically resolves.
- **Fertility/reproductive status:** There's no effect on sperm production or fertility. The frenulum is just a small band of tissue on the underside of the penis.

**Care while healing**

- Keep it clean with gentle warm water or mild saline soaks. Pat dry.
- Neosporin or another antibiotic ointment is fine in moderation, though some people react to the ingredients. Stop if there's a rash or irritation.
- **Avoid sex and masturbation** until it's fully healed (often 1-3 weeks, sometimes longer). Resuming too soon can re-tear it or lead to a scar.
- Watch for bleeding that won't stop, which may need pressure and medical attention.

**When to see a doctor**

- Bleeding that doesn't stop after 10-15 minutes of direct pressure
- Signs of infection (increasing redness, swelling, pus, fever, bad smell)
- It doesn't heal after a few weeks, or tears repeatedly
- Severe pain

**If it keeps tearing**

A short, tight frenulum (frenulum breve) can tear again. A minor procedure called a **frenuloplasty** or **frenulectomy**, or a circumcision, can fix it permanently. Using plenty of lubricant helps prevent future tears.

So don't worry. This is usually a minor and temporary setback. Congratulations on the upcoming wedding!
