In most cases, yes: once it has fully healed, most couples return to normal sex. Some men find the area a bit more sensitive or the skin tighter afterward. A small scar can form, and if it does it can occasionally cause discomfort or tear again more easily.

It won't cause erectile dysfunction. The frenulum is just a small band of tissue on the underside of the penis, and a tear doesn't affect the nerves or blood vessels that produce erections. If he has temporary trouble, it's usually anxiety about pain or re-injury, and that fades. There's no effect on sperm production or fertility, so his reproductive status won't change.

During healing, he should keep it clean with warm water or mild saline soaks and pat it dry. Neosporin or another antibiotic ointment is fine in moderation, though some people react to the ingredients, so he should stop if a rash or irritation shows up. He should skip sex and masturbation until it has fully healed. That's often 1-3 weeks, sometimes longer. Going back too soon can re-tear it or leave a scar.

See a doctor if:
- bleeding that won't stop with 10-15 minutes of direct pressure
- there are signs of infection (increasing redness, swelling, pus, fever, bad smell)
- the pain is severe
- it hasn't healed after a few weeks, or it tears again and again

A short, tight frenulum (frenulum breve) can tear over and over. A minor procedure called a frenuloplasty or frenulectomy can fix that permanently; circumcision does too. Plenty of lubricant helps prevent future tears.

So try not to worry; this is usually a minor, temporary setback. Congratulations on the wedding!
