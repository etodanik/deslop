The September 11 attacks (often called 9/11) were four coordinated terrorist attacks by the Islamist extremist group al-Qaeda against the United States on the morning of September 11, 2001.

Nineteen hijackers took control of four commercial airplanes. American Airlines Flight 11 was crashed into the North Tower of the World Trade Center in New York City, and United Airlines Flight 175 into the South Tower. American Airlines Flight 77 hit the Pentagon in Arlington, Virginia. United Airlines Flight 93 was headed for Washington, D.C., but passengers and crew fought back against the hijackers and it crashed into a field near Shanksville, Pennsylvania. Both Twin Towers collapsed within hours, and nearby buildings were destroyed or damaged.

Almost 3,000 people were killed, not counting the 19 hijackers, and more than 6,000 were injured. It was the deadliest terrorist attack in history. Many first responders have since fallen ill or died from illnesses connected to the toxic dust.

The aftermath:
- The US launched the War on Terror, invading Afghanistan in 2001 to target al-Qaeda and the Taliban, and Iraq in 2003.
- The Department of Homeland Security was created, and the USA PATRIOT Act expanded surveillance powers.
- Airport security was greatly increased, with the creation of the TSA.
- Osama bin Laden, the al-Qaeda leader who planned the attacks, was killed by US forces in Pakistan in 2011.
- The 9/11 Memorial and Museum and One World Trade Center now stand on the site in New York.

It's commemorated each year on September 11.
