The storm had finally exhausted itself by dawn, leaving the sea a restless gray and the air heavy with salt. Elias Marlow climbed down from the lighthouse, his knees protesting each step of the winding iron staircase, and pushed open the heavy door to survey the damage.

Seaweed lay strewn across the path. A shutter had been torn from the keeper's cottage and flung into the garden. Nothing unusual for a storm of that size. Elias had weathered thirty-one winters on this lonely stretch of rock, and he knew the sea's tempers as well as he knew his own.

But as he made his way down toward the water's edge, something caught his eye, wedged between two black boulders where the waves still licked and foamed. He stopped.

It was a boot.

He picked his way across the slick rocks and knelt to examine it. A man's boot, worn leather, the sole cracked and the laces still knotted tight, as though whoever had worn it had never untied it at all. It was not the sort of boot any fisherman in these parts would wear. The stitching was too fine, the leather too well made, and on the inside, in faded gold lettering, were the initials *T.H.*

Elias glanced up and down the empty shoreline. The only sound was the gulls and the slow pulse of the tide. Nobody had been out on those waters last night. Nobody could have been.

He turned the boot over in his hands, and something small slid out and clinked against the rock.
