I'm sorry about the joint pain. I'm not a doctor and can't diagnose you, but I can explain what these results usually mean.

ASO is antistreptolysin O, a blood test for antibodies your body makes after a streptococcal (strep) infection such as strep throat. A raised titre means you were probably exposed to group A streptococcus in the recent past; antibodies can stay high for weeks to months. A positive result alone doesn't confirm disease, though. Many healthy people, especially in certain regions, have high titres, so a high titre is not necessarily serious. The test is most useful when combined with symptoms and other tests.

With joint pain, the concern is rheumatic fever, which sometimes follows a strep infection when the immune system overreacts. It can cause joint pain that moves from joint to joint, fever, a rash, and sometimes heart involvement. Doctors diagnose it with a set of criteria called the Jones criteria. Your values (1:8, or around 1600 IU/mL) are on the high side, but your doctor will need to weigh them against the whole picture.

At 29, other causes are possible too: reactive arthritis, rheumatoid arthritis or other autoimmune conditions, overuse injuries or mechanical problems, vitamin D deficiency, viral infections, gout. Autoimmune and rheumatic conditions often run in families, so tell your doctor about your family history.

Useful tests include ESR and CRP (inflammation markers), rheumatoid factor, anti-CCP, ANA, a throat swab, and uric acid and vitamin D levels. If rheumatic fever is suspected, you'd also need an ECG and echocardiogram.

Whether it's curable depends on the diagnosis. For rheumatic fever, treatment is penicillin to clear any remaining strep bacteria, anti-inflammatory medicine for the joints, and sometimes long-term preventive penicillin to avoid recurrences. Joint symptoms generally resolve completely, though heart involvement needs follow-up. Other conditions are treated differently, and many can be well controlled.

See your doctor or a rheumatologist and ask about further testing. Go urgently if you have chest pain, shortness of breath, swelling, fever, or a rash.
