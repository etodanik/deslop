Probably not directly, and even if it could, it would likely be a bad idea for both you and the company. Much of it depends on what kind of debt it is, where you live, and how the company is structured.

The biggest problem is tax. If your company pays off your personal debt, the IRS (or your country's tax authority) will most likely treat the payment as one of three things: wages to you, taxable and subject to payroll taxes; a dividend or distribution, if you're an owner; or a loan to you, which should be documented with interest and repayment terms. The company usually can't deduct personal expenses as a business cost.

If you own a corporation or LLC, there's a second risk. Mixing personal and business money can weaken the "corporate veil", the limited-liability protection, and a court that treats you and the company as one entity lets your creditors go after business assets.

Could the company simply buy the debt, the way debt buyers purchase it from creditors at a discount? Technically yes. But if you control the company, regulators and tax authorities may see that as a sham, and a debt that's forgiven or bought at a discount can leave you with "cancellation of debt" income.

If you're an employee rather than an owner, your employer paying your debt is just extra compensation. Some employers do offer student loan repayment assistance, tax-free up to $5,250 per year in the US under certain rules.

What works better:

- Take a documented, interest-bearing loan from the company. Check the legal limits first, because public companies can't lend to executives in the US under Sarbanes-Oxley.
- Pay yourself a salary, bonus or distribution, pay the taxes on it, and use the money for the debt.
- Use an employer program, if there is one.

Because tax and legal consequences are involved, I'd talk to a CPA or attorney before doing anything. If you tell me the country, the type of company and the type of debt, I can be more specific.
