South Africa is a parliamentary republic, so voters don't elect the president directly the way Americans do.

Citizens vote for political parties in national elections held every five years. The National Assembly has 400 members, chosen by proportional representation: each party gets a share of the seats that matches its share of the vote. (Voters choose from party lists, and since recent reforms independent candidates can run too.) After the election the Assembly elects the President from among its members, usually the leader of the majority party or coalition, and that vote has to happen within 30 days. The President is both head of state and head of government, and can serve at most two five-year terms.

Below the national level it works like this:

- **National Council of Provinces:** represents provincial interests, and the provincial legislatures choose its delegates.
- **Provincial legislatures:** elected at the same time as the National Assembly, and they choose each province's Premier.
- **Local government:** municipal councils are elected through a mix of ward representatives and party lists.

In 2024 no party won an outright majority for the first time since 1994. The result was a coalition "Government of National Unity" and the re-election of Cyril Ramaphosa as president.
