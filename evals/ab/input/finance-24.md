Short answer: **probably not directly**, and even if it could, it would likely be a bad idea for both you and the company. The details depend on what kind of debt it is, where you live, and how the company is structured.

**Why it's complicated**

1. **Tax treatment.** If your company pays off your personal debt, the IRS (or your country's tax authority) will most likely treat it as either:
   - **Compensation/wages to you** (taxable income, subject to payroll taxes), or
   - **A shareholder distribution/dividend** (if you're an owner), or
   - **A loan to you** (which should be documented, with interest and repayment terms).
   The company usually can't deduct personal expenses as a business expense.

2. **Corporate formalities.** If you own a corporation or LLC, mixing personal and business money can weaken the 'corporate veil' (limited liability protection). Courts can treat the company and you as the same, so creditors could go after business assets.

3. **Buying the debt itself.** Technically, a company could purchase a debt (such as buying your debt from a creditor at a discount, as debt buyers do). But if you control the company, regulators and tax authorities might see this as a sham. Also, if the debt is forgiven or purchased at a discount, you may have 'cancellation of debt' income.

4. **Employer situation.** If you are an employee (not an owner), your employer paying your debt is just additional compensation, and some employers offer student loan repayment assistance (up to $5,250 tax-free per year in the US under certain rules).

**Better ways**

- Take a **documented, interest-bearing loan** from the company (check legal limits, since public companies can't lend to executives in the US under Sarbanes-Oxley).
- Pay yourself a **salary, bonus, or distribution** (with taxes paid) and use it to pay the debt.
- Use an employer program, if available.
