South Africa is a parliamentary republic, so its leaders aren't elected directly by voters in the way a US president is.

**The President**

- The President is both head of state and head of government.
- Citizens vote for political parties in national elections, held every five years.
- Parliament has a National Assembly of 400 members, who are chosen through a system of **proportional representation**. Each party gets a share of the seats that matches its share of the vote.
- After the election, the National Assembly **elects the President** from among its members, usually the leader of the majority party or coalition. This vote must happen within 30 days of the election.
- A President can serve a maximum of **two five-year terms**.

**Other levels**

- **National Assembly:** elected by proportional representation (voters choose from party lists, and since recent reforms, independent candidates can also run).
- **National Council of Provinces:** represents provincial interests; its delegates are chosen by provincial legislatures.
- **Provincial legislatures:** elected at the same time as the national assembly, and they choose each province's Premier.
- **Local government:** municipal councils are elected by a mix of ward representatives and party lists.

In 2024, no party won an outright majority for the first time since 1994, resulting in a coalition 'Government of National Unity' and the re-election of Cyril Ramaphosa as president.
